# Questions to write against — the owner intent sitting

> **Status:** `owner-guidance` · written 2026-08-28 for him, at his request:
> *"I would like your draft and some open ended questions first. Which I can
> then use as guidance and inspiration to write down what I mean properly."*
>
> **These are open on purpose.** No options, no A/B, nothing to pick. Answer in
> whatever length you like, skip anything that does not spark, and answer out of
> order — the order below is only a suggestion. **A one-line answer is a real
> answer**; so is *"I don't know yet"*, which is worth recording rather than
> guessing at later.
>
> **Where the answers go:** the estate-level ones become fleet-manager's global
> intent; the per-repo ones become that repo's own intent record. You do not
> have to format anything — write however you write, and a session turns it into
> the records without changing your words.
>
> **Why some of these look basic.** They are the slots the estate has never
> recorded for anything. The records are exhaustive about *state* and near-silent
> about *purpose*, because state is a by-product of working and purpose only ever
> comes from asking you.

## 1 · The whole thing — the layer above any single repo

This is the layer that is missing most, and answers here make many of the
per-repo questions unnecessary.

1. **Tell me how this started.** Not the first repo — the decision. What were
   you doing before, and what changed that made you start working this way?

2. **Describe a normal evening a year from now, if this is working.** What are
   you doing, what are the agents doing, and what are you *not* doing any more?
   Concrete beats abstract here.

3. **What would you be most disappointed never to have built?** It does not have
   to be something that exists yet.

4. **Which part of this do you genuinely enjoy, and which part do you tolerate?**
   The second half matters more — anything you tolerate is a candidate for
   agents to absorb, and nobody can guess which parts those are.

5. **If you could only keep three of the repositories, which three?** Not a
   proposal to cut anything — it is the fastest way to find out what is actually
   load-bearing to you, which the activity numbers cannot tell me.

6. **What made you decide to give agents this much freedom?** You have said it
   saves you time and that agents over-add walls. Was there a moment, or did it
   accumulate?

7. **What do you want to be true about this that isn't yet?** Deliberately vague
   — take it wherever it goes.

## 2 · The questions worth asking about any repo

Use these for whichever repo you feel like writing about. They map onto the
record template, but write freely — the mapping is a session's job.

1. **What were you thinking about when you started it?** The origin, however
   mundane. "I wanted to see if I could" is a complete answer.

2. **What did you imagine it would be, and how is it different now?** Where
   those diverge is usually where the intent got lost.

3. **Who did you picture using it?** Including "nobody, just me" — that is real
   information and the records currently assume it everywhere.

4. **What would make you proud of it?** Not the milestone — the thing that would
   make you glad you did it.

5. **What would make you walk away?** The estate records this for *nothing*.
   Agents have been deciding what earns its place using their own criterion
   because yours has never been written down.

6. **What do you keep thinking about when you're not working on it?** That is
   usually the real intent, and it is almost never what the documentation says
   the next task is.

### substrate-kit — *mostly answered already*
You have now said it is a real product that keeps existing, that done means
*correction-free*, that additions come only from new problems, and that it is
meant for others. What is still open:

- **What made you build it in the first place** — the moment, not the rationale.
  The record has your reason (*so agents take more initiative*) but not the
  trigger.
- **The house-style split:** at founding, the daily culture (markers, badges,
  born-red cards) was deliberately demoted to *"house style, not program law"*
  while the autonomy mechanics became binding for every adopter. That one choice
  is why the card ritual survived the program's close and the ideas loop died.
  Was it right?

## 4 · How you want to work

1. **What is the most annoying thing an agent does?** Be specific and petty —
   the small recurring irritations are the ones mechanisms can actually fix, and
   they never make it into a directive because they feel too small to say.

2. **When you check in after 30 minutes, what do you actually want to see?**
   Not what you tolerate — what would make the check-in feel worth it.

3. **What do you wish you could hand over completely but currently can't trust?**
   This is the highest-value answer in this section: it names the next thing
   worth building, in your terms rather than ours.

4. **When something goes wrong, what do you want to happen?** Stop and ask, fix
   it and tell you after, or record it and carry on — and does that change by
   how bad it is?
